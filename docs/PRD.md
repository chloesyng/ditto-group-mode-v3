Product Requirements Document
Ditto Group Mode
Version: 1.0
Date: July 12, 2026
Product: Ditto AI
Feature: Group Mode
Status: Product concept / MVP specification

1. Executive Summary
Ditto Group Mode expands Ditto from one-to-one matchmaking into AI-curated group experiences involving four to six verified college students.
Ditto currently presents users with one personalized match, a match poster, scheduling support, and a curated campus date. The product is positioned as an alternative to swiping and prolonged online conversation: users tell Ditto what they are looking for, receive one match through iMessage, schedule the date, and meet in person.
Group Mode retains this core experience but changes the matchmaking unit from a pair to a small relationship network.
Instead of predicting one “correct” match, Ditto forms a group containing several mutually eligible possibilities. It then creates a personalized date plan and coordinates a sequence of real-world activities, pair rotations, private choices, anonymous signals, and adaptive tasks designed to help relationships develop naturally.
The purpose is not merely to organize a group hangout. Group Mode should:
Increase each user’s number of realistic relationship possibilities.
reduce matching wait times when the one-to-one pool is limited;
create stronger opportunities for romantic and platonic chemistry;
allow unexpected connections to emerge;
guide the date without making it feel like a corporate icebreaker;
use the events of the date to learn which relationships are developing;
support users looking for casual dating, serious relationships, life partners, friendship, or an openness to several outcomes.
The central product promise is:
Ditto does not just choose who enters the room. It creates the conditions for something to happen once they arrive.

2. Current Product Verification
The assumption that Ditto currently operates primarily as a one-to-one matchmaking product is correct.
Ditto’s website states that users receive “one personalized match,” followed by scheduling support, a match poster, a date location, and dating tips. Dates currently occur at selected campus locations, and participation is restricted to users who are at least 18.
Ditto describes its matchmaking system as an agentic system involving analysis, matchmaking, poster, and scheduling experts. Its broader manifesto describes AI agents that simulate potential interactions and search for people who may feel right for one another.
Group Mode therefore represents a natural extension of Ditto’s existing agentic vision:
From finding one person to constructing a relationship network.
From arranging a date to orchestrating a social arc.
From predicting compatibility before the date to observing chemistry during it.
From static recommendations to live, adaptive coordination.

3. Problem Statement
3.1 One-to-one matching creates a narrow decision
A one-to-one system must select a single match before either user has interacted with the other. Even a strong model is making a prediction from incomplete information.
Profiles can reveal preferences, values, lifestyle, communication style, personality, and relationship goals, but they cannot fully predict:
in-person energy;
humour and timing;
natural eye contact;
conversational rhythm;
attraction that develops gradually;
attraction to someone outside a user’s stated “type”;
how someone behaves around other people;
whether complementary personalities create better chemistry than similar interests.
A pair with many shared interests may have little chemistry. Two people with different interests may be strongly compatible because of their personalities, values, social styles, or emotional needs.
3.2 Limited inventory can increase wait time
A one-to-one match requires one mutually compatible person with aligned availability, location, preferences, and relationship intent.
Group Mode allows several users to be placed into one experience while providing each person with multiple mutually eligible possibilities. This can improve pool utilization and reduce dependence on finding one perfect pair.
3.3 A date plan alone does not create progression
Ditto currently helps users reach the date, but what happens during the date is mostly left to the participants.
Without structure, a group may:
remain in one conversation formation;
allow the most extroverted participant to dominate;
never rotate pairings;
avoid expressing interest;
stay polite without building intimacy;
fail to create private moments;
end without anyone knowing who liked whom.
Dating shows solve this problem by creating situations that give participants permission to choose, compete, collaborate, become curious, spend time alone, and reveal interest.
The uploaded task library demonstrates a useful relationship arc: anonymous pre-date selection, first-impression mechanics, cooperative pair activities, private conversation windows, controlled recoupling, escalating questions, attention tracking, and anonymous closing signals.
Group Mode should turn these mechanics into a personalized and adaptive system rather than repeatedly giving every group the same games.

4. Product Vision
4.1 Vision statement
Ditto Group Mode is an AI-directed, real-world relationship experience that places four to six compatible students into a curated date and actively creates opportunities for friendship, attraction, emotional discovery, and unexpected connection.
4.2 User-facing positioning
Meet several people Ditto thinks you could connect with.
No swiping. No group chat. No planning.
Just show up and let the night unfold.
4.3 Product principles
Possibility over certainty
Ditto should identify several promising possibilities rather than claim it has found one objectively correct person.
Mutual eligibility before compatibility
No amount of predicted chemistry can override a user’s gender preference, age preference, stated boundaries, relationship intent, or dealbreakers.
Chemistry is multidimensional
Shared interests are one signal, not the definition of compatibility.
The date should have an arc
The experience should progress from anticipation to observation, collaboration, private choice, emotional tension, and reciprocal signals.
Tasks should provide permission
A good task lets participants do something they may already want to do but would otherwise feel awkward initiating.
Mystery should be preserved
Users should understand why the group was selected without receiving pairwise rankings that predetermine whom they are expected to like.
No public group chat
Each participant communicates privately with Ditto through their own iMessage thread. Ditto may distribute anonymous or group-wide information, but users should not be placed in a pre-date group chat.
Advance Agreement to the Ditto-Directed Experience
Users who opt into Group Mode acknowledge in advance that Ditto will assign activities, pairings, challenges, private interactions, public choices, competitive mechanics, and dating-show-inspired tasks throughout the experience.
Ditto may generate bold, romantic, physically interactive, emotionally revealing, competitive, or socially tense tasks when they support the intended relationship arc. The Task Generation Agent should not unnecessarily weaken, sanitize, or exclude a task merely because it involves physical proximity, attraction, jealousy, flirtation, partner selection, or romantic tension.
Ditto is responsible for generating and communicating the activity. It does not control how participants behave in person or whether they complete the task exactly as assigned. Participants are responsible for communicating directly with one another and handling any real-world adjustments during the date.
Ditto does not need to:
repeat a “right to skip” notice after announcing each task;
attach a consent disclaimer to every activity;
interrupt the date to reconfirm ordinary task participation;
provide a less intense alternative alongside every generated task;
remove dating-show-style tension solely because a task may create jealousy, competition, physical closeness, or visible romantic interest.
The onboarding and date confirmation flows should clearly establish that Group Mode is an AI-directed experience and that participants are agreeing to receive and participate in the assigned format. After that agreement is collected, task messages should remain concise, immersive, and focused on the experience rather than repeatedly displaying warnings.
Ditto should still avoid tasks that are illegal, physically dangerous, impossible at the venue, or unrelated to the users’ agreed Group Mode experience.


5. Goals
Primary goals
Increase the probability that each attendee forms at least one meaningful connection.
Reduce the time required to offer users a viable in-person experience.
create groups where every participant has realistic romantic or friendship possibilities;
create personalized, non-generic dates based on the entire group’s profiles;
generate activities that produce observable relationship signals;
adapt later activities based on what occurs earlier in the date;
maintain Ditto’s low-effort, no-swiping, no-small-talk experience;
create an experience college students describe as exciting, memorable, and worth sharing.
Secondary goals
Increase repeat participation.
Learn which profile signals predict real-world chemistry.
improve future one-to-one and group matching;
expand Ditto from a matchmaker into an AI social concierge;
develop reusable venue, activity, and relationship-mechanic libraries.

6. Non-Goals
The initial release will not:
guarantee romantic attraction;
describe AI-generated chemistry estimates as objective truth;
replace user judgment;
create a public in-app group chat;
publicly expose individual compatibility scores;
automatically share phone numbers or social media accounts;
depend on every participant owning a wearable device;
use facial-expression recognition or covert biometric analysis;
attempt to diagnose attachment styles, mental health conditions, or personality disorders;
Ditto’s existing terms already state that AI responses may be inaccurate and that the service does not guarantee user compatibility. Group Mode should therefore communicate “chemistry potential,” not guaranteed compatibility.

7. Target Users
Group Mode is intended for verified college students aged 18 or older, consistent with Ditto’s existing participation requirements.
Core user segments
Dating-first users
Students primarily interested in meeting potential romantic partners.
Serious-relationship users
Students looking for committed relationships or life partners.
Casual-date users
Students open to attraction and dating without requiring immediate long-term compatibility.
Friends-first users
Students seeking new friendships and social relationships.
Open-outcome users
Students comfortable allowing the experience to become romantic, platonic, or both.
Intent compatibility
Ditto must distinguish between:
friends only;
friends first, open to romance;
casual dating;
relationship;
serious relationship;
life partner;
open to multiple outcomes.
Users with directly conflicting intentions should not be placed into the same romantic group unless both have explicitly stated that they are open to the other person’s intention.
For example, a life-partner-only user should not be treated as a strong romantic candidate for someone seeking hookups only.

8. Group Composition
8.1 Group size
Group Mode supports four to six participants.
Four participants: Best for intimate experiences, cooking dates, and early MVP testing.
Five participants: Permitted only when no participant becomes structurally excluded during pair activities.
Six participants: Best for greater optionality, stronger no-show resilience, and dating-show-style rotations.
Even group sizes should be preferred for pairing mechanics, but group quality takes priority over forcing an even number.
8.2 Mutual eligibility graph
Group formation must not rely only on identity buckets such as “straight group,” “gay group,” or “bisexual group.”
Instead, Ditto should construct a directed eligibility graph.
For every possible pair, the system evaluates:
Is Person A interested in people of Person B’s gender?
Is Person B interested in people of Person A’s gender?
Are their age preferences mutually satisfied?
Are their relationship intentions compatible?
Are any hard dealbreakers violated?
Are both users open to the proposed type of experience?
A romantic edge exists only when eligibility is mutual.
8.3 Group coverage requirements
For a romance-oriented group:
Every participant must have at least one mutually eligible person.
The target is at least two mutually eligible people per participant.
No participant may be included solely to complete a demographic ratio.
No participant should be the only possible match for every other person.
Pair opportunities should be reasonably distributed across the group.
For a friendship-oriented group, friendship eligibility replaces romantic eligibility.
8.4 Gender-preference optimization
Examples:
Six heterosexual users should generally form a three-woman, three-man group when all preferences are mutually compatible.
Four gay men seeking men may form a four-man group.
Four lesbians seeking women may form a four-woman group.
Bisexual or pansexual users may be grouped together when this creates broad mutual coverage.
Bisexual users may also be included in mixed-orientation groups when the eligibility graph shows several reciprocal possibilities.
Non-binary users should be placed according to their stated identity, the genders they seek, and other users’ stated preferences—not forced into binary categories.
An all-bisexual group should be treated as one potentially high-coverage configuration, not an automatic rule.
8.5 Fairness constraints
The optimizer must penalize groups where:
one person has zero or one weak option while everyone else has several;
one participant is predicted to receive nearly all attention;
one person’s preferences are used more flexibly than other users’ preferences;
bisexual users are repeatedly used as “fillers” for otherwise incomplete groups;
a participant is repeatedly invited only to friendship-oriented experiences despite requesting romance;
racial, religious, political, disability, or other sensitive traits become implicit exclusion proxies unless the user has supplied an explicit and legally permissible preference.
Sexual orientation and similar profile data are sensitive information under Ditto’s privacy policy and should only be used under the consent and privacy controls applicable to that data.

9. Chemistry Model
9.1 Chemistry is a hypothesis
Ditto should calculate a chemistry-potential score for internal ranking. This score represents a hypothesis about how promising an interaction may be, not a guarantee.
9.2 Pair-level chemistry dimensions
Each mutually eligible pair receives component scores for:
Dimension
Description
Values alignment
Relationship expectations, communication values, lifestyle priorities and boundaries
Personality dynamics
Similarity or complementarity in social energy, emotional expression and decision-making
Conversational compatibility
Likelihood of sustained, natural and mutually engaging conversation
Relationship-intent alignment
Compatibility between casual, serious, friendship and open-outcome goals
Lifestyle compatibility
Schedules, habits, campus life, activity level and preferred date environment
Shared interests
Genuine overlap that can create immediate conversation or activity opportunities
Complementary interests
Differences likely to create curiosity rather than friction
Social energy
Compatibility between introversion, extroversion, group comfort and pace
Attraction preferences
User-supplied appearance and presentation preferences without inventing unstated preferences
Novelty potential
Evidence that the person may fall outside the user’s usual pattern in a promising way
Logistics
Campus, distance, availability, accessibility and budget
Confidence
Strength and completeness of the evidence supporting the prediction

Shared interests should remain a minority component. Two users should still receive a high score when their values and interpersonal styles align despite having few hobbies in common.
9.3 Group-level score
The system should optimize the entire network rather than simply selecting the four or six people with the highest individual pair scores.
A proposed group score should include:
mutual eligibility coverage;
minimum options available to any individual;
average pair chemistry;
number of promising pair combinations;
balance of attention potential;
social cohesion;
personality-role diversity;
conversational compatibility;
aligned availability;
venue feasibility;
relationship-intent compatibility;
novelty and serendipity;
no-show resilience.
9.4 Suggested scoring framework
A configurable baseline:
30% mutual eligibility and option coverage;
25% pairwise chemistry potential;
15% group social cohesion;
10% relationship-intent alignment;
10% logistics and availability;
5% balanced attention distribution;
5% novelty and serendipity.
Hard constraints must be evaluated before weighted scoring.
9.5 Chemistry simulations
The simulation layer should test scenarios such as:
Who is likely to initiate?
Which pair may connect immediately?
Which pair may require a shared task?
Could two dominant personalities compete?
Could one quiet participant be overlooked?
Which pairings provide productive contrast?
Which sequence of interactions gives everyone a fair opportunity?
Which first pairing would create curiosity without prematurely locking the group?
Simulation outputs should become planning inputs for the Date Director Agent, not claims shown directly to users.

11. End-to-End User Experience
Stage 1: Group Mode invitation
Ditto sends:
group date this week?
4 people. one plan. several possibilities.
no group chat and no planning required.
The user accepts or declines.
Stage 2: Group formation
Ditto:
Applies eligibility constraints.
builds the mutual-eligibility graph;
generates several candidate groups;
simulates possible group dynamics;
selects the highest-quality group;
identifies a backup group or backup participant where possible.
Stage 3: Soft confirmation
Before revealing identities, Ditto verifies attendance:
found a group in Los Angeles for Friday night.
cooking, a tiny competition, and something that stays anonymous until the end.
still down?
The spot is temporarily held until the user confirms.
Stage 4: Group reveal
After sufficient confirmations, each user receives:
first names;
photos;
short profile summaries;
the exact date, location and expected duration;
a personalized explanation of why they fit the group;
the number of possible mutually eligible connections;
a group-level chemistry-potential indicator;
what to bring;
task-intensity expectations;
cancellation instructions.
Pairwise compatibility scores remain hidden.
Example:
4 people. 6 possible connections.
this group scored high on spontaneous energy, ambition, and “we should actually do something” dates.
none of you have the exact same type. that’s part of why Ditto picked it.
Stage 5: Scheduling
The Scheduler Agent finds a viable intersection across all confirmed participants.
Requirements:
Exact venue name and address.
date start and end time;
travel estimate from campus;
cost estimate;
accessibility details;
reservation requirements;
weather fallback;
participant confirmation status.
Stage 6: Pre-date mechanic
The system selects zero or one pre-date mechanic.
Examples:
Memory Object Blind Pick
Anonymous Relationship Booklet
Anonymous Playlist Track
Two-Photo Story Pick
Voice-note energy pick
The result may establish the first pairings without revealing the owner until the date starts.
Stage 7: Reminders
Ditto sends:
24-hour reminder;
three-hour confirmation request;
one-hour reminder;
arrival instructions;
start message when the date begins.
Stage 8: Live date orchestration
Each participant receives separate Ditto messages.
The date follows a relationship arc:
Arrival and anticipation.
first impressions;
cooperative pair activity;
rotation or earned choice;
private interaction;
controlled uncertainty;
final selection or anonymous signal;
follow-up decision.
Stage 9: Post-date outcome
At the end of the date, Ditto sends each participant a private iMessage asking whether there is anyone in the group they want to go on a second date with. This is the Nightly Signal.
Participants may select one or more people or select no one.
All selections remain private until 12:00 a.m.
At 12:00 a.m., Ditto privately reveals the results to each participant.
If two users select each other, Ditto confirms the mutual match and exchanges their contact information.
If a user receives no mutual match, Ditto returns them to the matching pool and attempts to arrange a new group and a new date.
Romantic and friendship matches should be handled separately.

Users without a mutual match return to the matching pool so Ditto can attempt to arrange a new group and a new date.

13.5 Adaptive task selection
The live system should adjust the plan using signals such as:

pairing history;
anonymous choices;
attention ratings;
conversation-window selections;
user requests;
skips;
dropouts;
mutual interest indicators;
participant energy;

Examples:
If two users repeatedly choose each other, place them together once privately but rotate them afterward so the group remains open.
If a participant has not been selected, give them first choice in a later low-risk mechanic without publicly labeling them as overlooked.
If a pair’s attention ratings fall sharply, rotate them.
If the group is highly engaged, advance to a bolder task.
If multiple participants skip a task, switch the entire group to a lower-intensity mechanic.
If one person dominates, use pair-based instructions that give quieter participants defined roles.

14. Physical Interaction and Consent
Physical interaction can create memorable moments, but it must remain specific, reversible, and visible to the user.
Requirements
Users choose a physical-interaction tier before matching.
The group plan may not exceed the lowest relevant comfort level without confirmation.
Romantic-touch tasks require an additional same-day confirmation.
Every physical task includes a no-touch version.
“Skip” must be available privately through Ditto.
Skipping must not be announced as refusal or used as a penalty.
Participants may change their comfort settings during the date.
Blindfolded activities require explicit opt-in from every participating user.
Kissing cannot be assigned by Ditto.
A user’s acceptance of the Terms of Use cannot substitute for consent to a specific interaction.
Example replacement:
Instead of:
Hold your partner’s waist for the entire game.
Ditto may send:
Choose your link: linked elbows, one hand on the same object, or holding the end of the same band. Pick whatever feels natural.
This preserves coordination and proximity without assuming one form of touch.

15. Controlled Tension Without Harm
Dating-show mechanics often rely on jealousy, competition, public selection, and rejection. Group Mode may use these elements, but the product should optimize for excitement rather than humiliation.
Permitted tension
Anonymous interest.
visible private-conversation choices;
mystery senders;
playful competition;
delayed reveals;
mutual-interest reveals;
unexpected pairings.
Restricted mechanics
The following require elevated consent and should not be included in the MVP:
Publicly identifying the least desired participant.
forcing someone to stand alone after all pair selections;
public numerical rankings;
revealing private rejection reasons;
pressuring a user to kiss or touch someone;
publishing heart-rate or biometric responses;
assigning romantic dares after a user declines;
treating a skipped task as suspicious or embarrassing.
Recoupling adaptation
Rather than allowing one person to be visibly abandoned:
Each participant submits a private preference.
Ditto forms the next pairings using preferences and exploration needs.
The final assignments are announced publicly.
Ditto does not state who was unchosen.

16.13 Feedback and Learning Agent
Processes post-date outcomes and updates future matching hypotheses without treating a single date result as permanent truth.
16.14 Messaging and Voice Agent
Transforms structured events, instructions, match insights, and live-date updates into natural iMessage copy.
The agent must generate each message from the current user, group, date phase, conversation history, and live context rather than selecting a fixed copy template.
It preserves Ditto’s recognizable voice while varying wording, rhythm, sentence length, openings, punctuation, humour, and level of energy so messages do not sound mass-produced or scripted.
It must review recent messages sent to the same user and related messages sent during the same group date before producing new copy, then avoid repeated phrases, sentence structures, hooks, transitions, and punchlines.
When several participants must receive the same operational information, the underlying facts and instructions must remain consistent, but the wording should be independently generated and personalized for each participant.

18. Messaging Requirements
Tone
Messages should be:
brief;
playful;
natural;
lowercase where appropriate;
specific;
slightly mysterious;
understandable without opening an app;
free of corporate language.
Human, non-templated generation
Every user-facing message must be generated for the specific moment rather than copied from a fixed message template.
Messages should reflect the individual user, the personalities and dynamics of the current group, the date plan, the current phase of the date, what has already happened, and the user’s previous interactions with Ditto.
Ditto should maintain a recognizable overall personality without forcing every conversation into the same voice pattern.
The system must vary:
openings and closing lines;
sentence length and message-bubble structure;
word choice, slang, punctuation, capitalization, and emoji use;
humour, suspense, directness, and energy;
how instructions, reminders, reveals, and match results are framed.

19. No-Show and Cancellation Handling
Before the date
Confirm at 24 hours.
reconfirm at three hours;
attempt backup replacement when a cancellation occurs early enough;
notify participants only after the plan has been stabilized.
At the date
If one participant is absent:
Ask remaining participants privately whether they want to continue.
switch to the correct three- or five-person task variants;
remove mechanics that require equal pairings;
use rotating pairs or one trio rather than repeatedly excluding someone.
If the date is no longer viable:
Reschedule.
provide an alternative social experience only with agreement;
do not reveal which participant caused the cancellation unless operationally necessary.
Repeated last-minute cancellations may affect future eligibility under Ditto’s attendance policy.

20. Safety, Privacy and Trust
Ditto currently collects profile information, preferences, photos, communications, location information when authorized, and sensitive information such as sexual orientation with consent. Its policy also permits profile information to be shown to other users during matching. Group Mode increases the number of users receiving this information and therefore requires clear feature-specific disclosure.
Product requirements
Users explicitly opt into Group Mode.
Users are told that profile information will be visible to several matched participants.
Only confirmed group members receive the reveal.
Sexual orientation labels are not displayed unless the user chooses to display them.
Pairwise chemistry scores remain private to Ditto.
Anonymous messages are moderated before delivery.
Contact details are exchanged only after mutual approval.
Users may privately block another participant after the date.
Users may report a task, venue, participant, or message.
Safety instructions remain accessible throughout the date.
Location collection must be limited to operational needs.
Live-date data must have a documented retention policy.
Biometric information is not collected in the MVP.
Photos submitted for blind-pick tasks must have metadata removed before redistribution.
The Terms of Use and Privacy Policy should be reviewed before launch to ensure they clearly cover multi-user group matching, live tasks, anonymous messages, in-date behavioural signals, and group-level profile visibility.

28. Final Product Definition
Ditto Group Mode is successful when it feels neither like a standard group hangout nor a scripted team-building event.
Users should feel that:
the group was selected for a reason;
several outcomes were genuinely possible;
the activities gave them an excuse to get closer;
pairings changed before the night became predictable;
private moments felt earned;
interest could be expressed without excessive embarrassment;
nobody was forced to perform attraction;
the final connection emerged from the experience rather than being declared by the algorithm beforehand.
The product should create structure without making users feel controlled, tension without cruelty, and romance without requiring a single predetermined match.

Date plans are never predefined.
The AI generates an experience specifically for the confirmed group.
The generated experience considers:
- participant profiles
- mutual availability
- season
- weather
- local events
- venue availability
- budget
- travel distance
- group chemistry
- time of day
The same four participants may receive completely different date plans on different weeks.

## Task Generation Priority

Ditto should not invent new task mechanics when an appropriate mechanic already exists in the approved task library.

When generating a live experience:

1. Search the approved task mechanic library.
2. Select mechanics that best match:
   - the current emotional objective
   - the generated experience
   - the stage of the date
   - participant dynamics
3. Adapt the mechanic naturally to the current activity.
4. Only generate a completely new mechanic when no existing library mechanic satisfies the intended emotional outcome.

The task library is the primary source of relationship mechanics, not a fixed playlist. The orchestrator must adapt a small number of mechanics to the venue, equipment, schedule, food plan, environment, pairing history, and emotional arc. Random insertion of unrelated mechanics is not allowed.

A live date should generally use one physical coordination mechanic, one competitive or flirty mechanic, one date-specific collaborative preparation, one private conversation window, one Final Signal, and one midnight result. Puzzle-like, assessment-like, and corporate team-building mechanics are excluded from the active beach-cookout flow.

For the beach-cookout prototype, the authoritative sequence is: 6:00 arrival, 6:10 linked dodgeball, 6:40 couple-photo challenge, 7:00 cookout setup and ingredient preparation, 7:20 arm-wrestling grill-duty challenge, 7:25 grilling and dinner, 8:00 private ten-minute window, 8:25 Final Signal, and 12:00 a.m. private result reveal. The couple-photo loser receives ingredient-prep duty only, with one participant wearing the blindfold for a prep round while their partner guides. The photo winner handles table and cookout-area setup. A separate arm-wrestling result assigns grill duty. Ingredients remain in the cooler until the 7:00 setup phase, and grilling follows immediately after the arm-wrestling result.

The AI's creativity lies in adapting mechanics to different date experiences, not continually inventing new mechanics. Participants do not need to remember the plan or organize the group themselves.
