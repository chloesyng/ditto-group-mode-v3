# Ditto Group Mode — Task Mechanic Library

## Purpose

This document is the approved source of truth for pre-date assignments, live date mechanics, private signals, pairing privileges, and closing mechanics used by Ditto Group Mode.

Ditto should reference this library before generating any new task.

The AI’s role is to:

1. identify the emotional objective of the current date phase
2. select the strongest compatible mechanic from this library
3. adapt that mechanic to the confirmed group and generated date experience
4. preserve the mechanic’s core relationship effect
5. generate a new mechanic only when no approved option is suitable

The goal is not to keep participants busy.

The goal is to create:

- curiosity
- trust
- playful dependence
- physical closeness
- competition
- visible preference
- jealousy
- emotional tension
- private intimacy
- meaningful romantic choice

---

## Global Rules

### Mutual Romantic Eligibility

Any romantic pairing, anonymous selection, private invitation, or attraction signal must respect mutual romantic eligibility.

A participant may only be shown or paired with another participant when:

- the viewer’s preferences include the other participant
- the other participant’s preferences include the viewer
- both participants are eligible to match with one another

Do not filter using gender alone.

---

### Task Selection Priority

When selecting a mechanic, Ditto must consider:

- current date phase
- intended emotional outcome
- participant profiles
- relationship intentions
- mutual eligibility
- pairing history
- previous signals
- venue and environment
- date duration
- available equipment
- weather
- accessibility
- consent
- safety
- whether the mechanic has already been used

Do not repeatedly use the same limitation, challenge structure, or emotional mechanic during one date unless repetition is intentional.

The library is a collection of reusable social mechanics, not a script or checklist. The date orchestrator must adapt only a small number of strong mechanics to the confirmed venue, activity, equipment, schedule, food plan, environment, pairing history, and intended emotional arc. Random insertion of unrelated mechanics is not allowed.

A strong selected mechanic should create at least two of: bonding, reliance, safe proximity, curiosity, light jealousy, observable chemistry, pair disruption, or a consequence that affects a later moment. Puzzle-like, trivia-based, assessment-like, and corporate team-building mechanics should be avoided. A date should contain only enough guided activity to shape the social arc, with unstructured time preserved between meaningful moments.

---

### Adaptation Rule

Preserve the mechanic’s core relationship effect when adapting it.

For example:

A physical team-game mechanic may be adapted from dodgeball into a target game, balance game, reaction game, or passing game.

However, the adapted version must still preserve:

- partner dependence
- physical connection
- competition
- clear rules
- a meaningful reward

Do not reduce strong mechanics into generic activities such as:

- find an item
- prepare something
- solve a puzzle
- choose supplies
- discuss a topic

A basic activity must be paired with the actual relationship mechanic that makes it emotionally engaging.

### Active Beach-Cookout Adaptation

The active beach sequence uses linked dodgeball at 6:10 PM, the couple-photo challenge at 6:40 PM, ingredient prep and cookout setup at 7:00 PM, one arm-wrestling match at 7:20 PM, and grilling at 7:25 PM. The photo loser receives ingredient-prep duty only. One member of that pair must wear the blindfold for a prep round while their partner guides. The photo winner handles table and cookout-area setup. Grill duty is decided separately by arm wrestling and must never be inherited automatically from the photo result.

The prep message should remain concise and playful. Internal safety constraints belong in mechanic data rather than a long user-facing lecture. The arm-wrestling representatives are chosen in person; **Match finished** is the only interface action.

---

### Privacy Rules

Mid-date anonymous signals must remain anonymous.

Do not reveal:

- who selected whom
- who sent an anonymous message
- whether a selection was mutual
- attraction rankings
- signal counts
- popularity

Mutual mid-date signals may influence later pairings, but they must not be revealed.

The Final Signal is handled separately and may reveal mutual results at midnight.

---

### Consent and Safety

Any mechanic involving physical contact, restricted communication, reduced vision, reduced hearing, linked movement, or physical limitation must:

- be easy to stop immediately
- match the environment
- respect accessibility needs

Create opportunities for closeness.

---

### Mechanics Versus Activities

The activity is the setting.

The mechanic creates the relationship effect.

Example:

Activity:

Eating a shared course.

Mechanic:

Partners remain loosely connected at the wrist, making ordinary actions less convenient and creating natural opportunities to assist one another.

Example:

Activity:

Outdoor target game.

Mechanic:

Partners must remain physically linked. One attacks while the other protects. Separating eliminates the pair.

Example:

Activity:

Cooking.

Mechanic:

One participant controls preparation while the other controls cooking. Neither can complete the dish independently.

---

## Standard Mechanic Format

Every approved mechanic should use this structure:

### Mechanic Name

Pre-date / Early / Mid / Late / Multi-phase / Closing.

**Emotional Objective:**  
The relationship effect this mechanic is intended to create.

**Core Mechanic:**  
What happens.

**Why It Works:**  
Why the mechanic changes the participants’ dynamic.

**Eligibility:**  
Required relationship intentions, group structures, or participant conditions.

**Environment Requirements:**  
Required venue, equipment, space, duration, or technology.

**Pairing Rules:**  
How participants are selected and how mutual eligibility is preserved.

**Interaction Level:**  
None / conversational / cooperative / physical / chosen contact.

**Privacy and Reveal Rules:**

**Consent and Safety:**

**Codex Generation Instructions:**  
Exact instructions for adapting and implementing the mechanic.

**Do Not:**  
Mistakes Codex must avoid.

# Approved Mechanics

## Memory Object Blind Pick

**ID:**  
`pre_memory_object_blind_pick`

**Arc Timing:**  
Pre-date.

**Emotional Objective:**  
Create anticipatory curiosity and emotional projection before participants meet.

The participant should become interested in someone’s taste, memories, or inner world before seeing that person in the room.

**Core Mechanic:**  
Each participant privately submits one photo of a meaningful place, object, or moment.

The photo is shown anonymously to mutually eligible participants.

Each participant selects the submission they feel most curious about.

The selection may influence the first pairing of the date.

**Why It Works:**  
Participants form an emotional impression before appearance and live chemistry take over.

The anonymous choice makes the eventual reveal feel intentional and slightly fated.

The person arrives already curious about the story behind the selected image.

**Eligibility:**  
Use only between mutually romantically eligible participants.

A participant should only see submissions from people when:

- their preferences include the submitter
- the submitter’s preferences include them
- both participants are eligible to match with one another

For a heterosexual group with two women and two men:

- each woman sees only the two men’s submissions
- each man sees only the two women’s submissions

Do not filter by gender alone.

**Environment Requirements:**  
No physical venue requirement.

Requires:

- private image upload
- anonymous image display
- participant-selection interface
- secure storage of submission ownership
- removal of identifying metadata

**Pairing Rules:**  
The selections influence the first complete pairing structure for the group.

Ditto should prioritize:

1. mutual selections
2. one-sided selections that produce the strongest complete arrangement
3. remaining mutually eligible pairings

Every participant must receive exactly one partner for the first paired phase.

Do not resolve each selection independently if doing so leaves another participant without an eligible partner.

**Interaction Level:**  
None before the date.

The mechanic creates anticipatory chemistry rather than physical interaction.

**Privacy and Reveal Rules:**  
Before the date, do not reveal:

- who submitted each photo
- who selected whom
- whether a selection was mutual
- how the pairing was calculated

At the beginning of the date, Ditto may reveal:

- whose submission the participant selected
- who they are paired with for the first phase

Do not reveal who selected the participant unless another approved mechanic explicitly requires it.

**Rewards or Consequences:**  
The result influences the first pairing.

It may also influence:

- the first paired challenge
- the first private conversation opportunity
- later pairing priority

The selection does not create a final romantic match.

**Consent and Safety:**  
Do not request:

- intimate images
- identifying documents
- precise home addresses
- private medical information
- sexual content
- photos that expose another person without consent

Participants may submit a non-personal object or place.

They may skip the optional caption.

**Codex Generation Instructions:**  
Prompt each participant to submit:

- one photo
- one optional caption with a maximum of 10 words

Strip:

- names
- profile information
- identifying metadata
- original filenames when necessary

Show each participant only mutually eligible submissions.

Store:

- submission owner
- viewer
- selection
- mutual-selection status
- resulting group pairing

Generate the first complete pairing assignment after every participant has selected or skipped.

At date start, send a private reveal such as:

“You picked Olivia’s photo.”

Then send the first pairing assignment.

**Do Not:**  

- show submissions from romantically incompatible participants
- reveal the submitter before the live date
- tell participants that a selection was mutual
- treat the first click as a final match
- use appearance-based profile information beside the anonymous images
- generate pairings that break the group’s mutual eligibility structure
- ask for a long written explanation

## Anonymous Relationship Booklet

**ID:**  
`pre_anonymous_relationship_booklet`

**Arc Timing:**  
Pre-date.

**Emotional Objective:**  
Create attraction through values, judgment, and relationship mindset before appearance becomes the main signal.

The participant should feel:

“I like how this person thinks.”

**Core Mechanic:**  
Each participant answers five short relationship-dilemma questions privately.

Their answers are combined into an anonymous digital booklet.

Each participant reviews booklets from mutually eligible participants and selects the one they feel most aligned with or curious about.

The selection may influence the first pairing.

**Why It Works:**  
Participants project a personality onto an anonymous voice.

They begin forming interest based on values and decision-making rather than appearance.

The eventual identity reveal becomes an emotional payoff.

**Eligibility:**  
Use only for participants whose stated relationship intention supports deeper compatibility exploration.

Recommended for:

- serious relationships
- long-term relationships
- life-partner intentions

Do not use by default for participants seeking:

- casual dating
- low-commitment experiences
- primarily activity-based social dates

All booklet distribution must preserve mutual romantic eligibility.

A participant should only receive booklets from people they are eligible to match with.

**Environment Requirements:**  
No venue requirement.

Requires:

- private written-response collection
- anonymous booklet generation
- eligible-participant filtering
- selection tracking
- secure answer storage

**Question Generation Rules:**  
Generate five dilemmas based on the participant’s stated values, boundaries, and relationship goals.

Questions should reveal differences in judgment without creating distress.

Possible topics include:

- first-date expectations
- communication frequency
- exclusivity timing
- conflict repair
- family introductions
- privacy in relationships
- money and planning
- independence versus togetherness
- social media boundaries
- balancing career and relationships

Questions should be:

- short
- specific
- answerable in one to three sentences
- open enough to reveal personality
- safe for anonymous sharing

**Pairing Rules:**  
After every participant reviews the eligible booklets, Ditto should prioritize:

1. mutual booklet selections
2. strong one-sided selections that create the best complete group pairing
3. remaining mutually eligible combinations

The final result must create a complete valid pairing structure.

Do not assign pairings independently if doing so strands another participant.

**Interaction Level:**  
None before the date.

The mechanic creates intellectual and emotional anticipation.

**Privacy and Reveal Rules:**  
Before the date, do not reveal:

- the booklet author
- who selected whom
- whether a selection was mutual
- compatibility scores
- how the pairing was resolved

At the live-date reveal, Ditto may disclose:

- whose booklet the participant selected
- the resulting first partner

Do not reveal who selected the participant unless a separate approved mechanic requires it.

**Rewards or Consequences:**  
The result may influence:

- the first pairing
- the first private activity
- early conversation context
- later compatibility testing

The booklet choice is not a final romantic decision.

**Consent and Safety:**  
Do not generate questions involving:

- trauma
- abuse
- sexual history
- medical information
- family estrangement
- financial hardship
- immigration status
- religion or politics unless explicitly relevant and voluntarily provided
- deeply personal disclosures

Participants may skip any question.

Skipped answers should not be penalized.

**Codex Generation Instructions:**  
For each participant:

1. Read their application answers and relationship intention.
2. Confirm this mechanic is appropriate for their stated goal.
3. Generate five calibrated relationship dilemmas.
4. Collect one short written answer per dilemma.
5. Build one anonymous booklet object.
6. Remove names, photos, schools, majors, and identifying details.
7. Show the booklet only to mutually eligible participants.
8. Record each selection.
9. Resolve one complete first-pairing structure for the group.
10. Store the author, viewer, selection, and mutual-selection status privately.

At the live-date reveal, send a message such as:

“You picked Olivia’s booklet.”

Then reveal the first pairing.

**Do Not:**

- use the mechanic for every group
- show booklets from incompatible participants
- generate generic personality-test questions
- ask all participants the exact same five questions when personalization is possible
- reveal mutual selections
- include identifying details inside the booklet
- treat the booklet selection as a final match
- force participants to answer sensitive questions

## Postcard Pick

**ID:** `early_postcard_pick`

**Stage:** Pre-date submission with an early-date reveal

**Core purpose:**  
Creates curiosity before participants meet by asking each person to choose an anonymous meaningful image from someone they are mutually eligible to match with.

The live reveal gives that earlier choice a clear payoff and may shape the first pairing.

**Best for:**  
Groups where participants are comfortable submitting a photo of a meaningful place, object, or moment and where every participant has at least one mutually eligible option.

**Avoid when:**  
A participant has opted out of pre-date submissions, there are not enough eligible submissions to preserve anonymity, or submitted images contain identifying or sensitive information that cannot be safely removed.

**Mechanic:**  
Before the date, each participant privately submits:

- One photo of a meaningful place, object, or moment
- One optional caption with a maximum of 10 words

After all submissions are received, Ditto removes identifying information and privately shows each participant only the anonymous submissions belonging to mutually romantically eligible participants.

Each participant selects the image they are most curious about.

Suggested submission prompt:

> Send me one photo of a place, object, or moment that means something to you.
>
> Nothing too serious. Just something with a story behind it.

Suggested selection prompt:

> Everyone sent something in.
>
> Pick the one you are most curious about.
>
> Do not overthink it.

The owner remains hidden until the date begins.

At the live-date reveal, Ditto may say:

> Remember the postcard you picked?
>
> It was Olivia's.

Ditto may then use the selection to create the first complete pairing configuration.

**Why it works:**  
Participants become curious about someone's taste, memories, or inner world before appearance and live chemistry take over.

The anonymous choice gives the first pairing emotional context without presenting it as a compatibility score or final match.

**Task flow:**

1. Confirm that the participant opted into pre-date photo submissions.
2. Collect one private image and optional short caption from each participant.
3. Remove filenames, metadata, names, addresses, and identifying details.
4. Validate that every image is safe and appropriate to redistribute anonymously.
5. Build each participant's eligible submission set using mutual romantic eligibility.
6. Show only eligible anonymous submissions.
7. Allow one private selection.
8. Store the submission owner, viewer, selection, and mutual-selection status.
9. Generate one complete valid first-pairing configuration after all responses close.
10. Keep ownership and mutuality hidden before the date.
11. At the beginning of the date, reveal the selected image's owner privately.
12. Announce the first pairing without explaining the full pairing logic.

**Pairing logic:**

Prioritize:

1. Mutual postcard selections
2. One-sided selections that produce the strongest complete valid configuration
3. Underexplored eligible pairings
4. Pairing balance across the entire group

For a four-person group, every participant must receive exactly one partner.

Do not resolve each selection independently if that would leave another participant without a valid partner.

The postcard choice influences the first pairing but does not automatically guarantee that the viewer is paired with the image owner.

**Eligibility rules:**

A submission may appear only when:

- The viewer's preferences include the submitter
- The submitter's preferences include the viewer
- Both participants satisfy all other romantic eligibility constraints

Do not infer eligibility from gender alone.

Do not show submissions from incompatible participants merely to create more options.

**Privacy and reveal rules:**

Before the live date, do not reveal:

- Submission owner
- Profile photo
- Name
- Gender label
- School or major
- Original filename
- Image metadata
- Who selected whom
- Whether a selection was mutual
- How the pairing was calculated

At the beginning of the date, Ditto may reveal:

- Whose image the participant selected
- The participant's first partner
- The next action

Do not reveal who selected the participant's own image unless another approved mechanic explicitly permits it.

**Image rules:**

Approved submissions may include:

- A landscape
- A meaningful public place
- A personal object
- A hobby-related object
- A meal or ordinary moment
- A non-identifying memory image

Reject or request a replacement when the image contains:

- A visible face without appropriate consent
- A precise home address
- Identifying documents
- Readable private messages
- Medical information
- Sexual content
- Illegal content
- Another person's private information
- Metadata that cannot be safely removed

**Interaction rules:**

- Keep the assignment quick and visually simple.
- Do not ask for a long explanation.
- The caption is optional.
- Participants may submit a non-personal object or place.
- Do not present the mechanic as a personality test.
- Do not use appearance-based profile information beside anonymous images.
- Do not reveal whether the pick was mutual.
- Do not treat the selection as a final romantic choice.

**Codex implementation notes:**

- Keep the ID `early_postcard_pick`.
- Treat submission as a pre-date interaction and ownership reveal as an early live-date interaction.
- Store image ownership separately from the anonymized display asset.
- Strip EXIF metadata and replace original filenames before redistribution.
- Filter visible submissions using the structured pair-based mutual-eligibility map.
- Support one selection, a skipped submission, and a skipped selection.
- Store:
  - `submission_owner_id`
  - `viewer_id`
  - `selected_submission_id`
  - `mutual_selection`
  - `resulting_pair_configuration`
  - `revealed_at`
- Generate the first complete pairing only after the response window closes.
- Re-display the exact selected image during the live reveal.
- Never expose mutuality or internal pairing scores.

### The MBTI Read (Retired From Active Date Orchestration)

This mechanic is retained for historical reference only. It must not be selected for the active beach-cookout flow or inserted into future dates as a generic personality assessment.

**ID:** `early_mbti_read`

**Stage:** Early date

**Core purpose:**  
Creates playful observation and low-stakes teasing by making the group read each other before they know one another too well.

**Best for:**  
Groups where multiple participants included MBTI or personality-related answers in their applications.

**Avoid when:**  
Fewer than three participants have usable personality information, or the answers would reveal sensitive/private application details.

**Mechanic:**  
Ditto selects one participant and releases one MBTI dimension at a time without initially revealing whose profile it belongs to.

For example:

- Introvert or extrovert
- Intuitive or sensing
- Thinking or feeling
- Judging or perceiving

After each dimension is revealed, the group discusses and guesses who it belongs to.

Once the final guess is locked in, Ditto reveals the person and may share one short, application-grounded explanation for why the result fits them.

**Task flow:**

1. Select one participant with a known MBTI result.
2. Release the first personality dimension.
3. Ask everyone to privately submit their guess.
4. Release the next dimension and allow guesses to change.
5. Continue until all four dimensions are shown.
6. Reveal the correct participant.
7. Optionally reveal who guessed correctly or who changed their answer the most.

**Interaction rules:**

- Keep the tone playful rather than diagnostic.
- Do not treat MBTI as objective science or use it to claim romantic compatibility.
- Never expose the participant’s full application response.
- Do not repeat the mechanic for every participant during the same date.
- The reveal should create conversation, not become a lengthy personality quiz.

**Codex implementation notes:**

- Only select this task when sufficient personality data exists.
- Fire during the early stage, after initial introductions but before stronger romantic tasks.
- Send each MBTI dimension as a separate timed message.
- Store each participant’s private guess after every reveal.
- Allow guesses to be updated until the final dimension is released.
- Reveal the selected participant to everyone at the end.
- Use their actual application data; never randomly assign an MBTI type.

### Earn Your Paradise

**ID:** `early_earn_your_paradise`

**Stage:** Early date

**Core purpose:**  
Creates a playful competition that gives the group a shared goal while naturally revealing chemistry, teamwork, and who gravitates toward whom.

**Best for:**  
Groups that respond well to light competition, movement, teamwork, or playful challenges.

**Avoid when:**  
The venue has limited space, the group has accessibility constraints that make the selected challenge unsuitable, or the task would disrupt the public setting.

**Mechanic:**  
Ditto gives the group a short challenge. The reward is a desirable date advantage, referred to as “Paradise.”

The reward should meaningfully affect the date, such as:

- First choice of partner for the next eligible task
- First choice of paired role
- Best seats for the next phase
- Choosing the group's next dessert, drink, song, or activity
- Unlocking one private clue
- Choosing who receives the next eligible side conversation
- Receiving limited control over the next pairing

The challenge may be individual, paired, or group-based depending on the date setting.

**Challenge examples:**

- Complete a quick coordination challenge in pairs
- Build or arrange something using limited materials
- Win a short trivia round based on clues revealed during the date
- Complete a photo scavenger task around the venue
- Recreate a pose, object, or scene using the available surroundings
- Finish a small food-preparation challenge first
- Solve a short puzzle using information distributed among all four people

**Task flow:**

1. Select one venue-compatible challenge from an approved mechanic template.
2. Define one clear Paradise reward that affects the next part of the date.
3. Announce the challenge, time limit, winning criteria, and reward.
4. Confirm that the challenge fits accessibility, safety, available materials, and venue rules.
5. Run the challenge.
6. Determine the winner using the stated criteria.
7. Apply the reward immediately.
8. Store the outcome and prevent repeated structural advantages.

**Interaction rules:**

- Keep the challenge under 10 minutes.
- The reward must affect the date rather than becoming a meaningless badge.
- Explain how the winner is determined before the task starts.
- The challenge should create interaction between participants rather than four separate phone actions.
- Do not base the challenge on embarrassment, physical attractiveness, alcohol consumption, or private information.
- Do not use a highly athletic challenge unless every participant indicated comfort with that activity.
- Do not let a reward override romantic eligibility, participant boundaries, safety, or valid remaining-pair logic.
- Do not repeatedly award the same participant structural control.
- Do not make the losing pair feel punished or publicly undesirable.

**Reward logic:**

Approved rewards may affect:

- Pairing order
- Paired role selection
- Seating
- Music
- Dessert or a shared item
- A private clue
- The order of the next activity
- A future side-conversation opportunity

A reward must not:

- Expose private rankings
- Force an incompatible pairing
- Transfer contact information
- Override a decline or opt-out
- Control the Final Signal
- Publicly label a participant as more desirable

**Fallback logic:**

If the planned challenge becomes impossible:

- Fall back to a seated puzzle, observation challenge, or conversation-supported cooperative task.
- Preserve the same reward when possible.
- Keep the fallback similar in duration and effort.
- Do not invent venue access, equipment, permits, or operating conditions.

**Codex implementation notes:**

- Use the ID `early_earn_your_paradise`.
- Select the challenge using venue, mobility, weather, preferences, current energy, and available-material data.
- Generate the challenge from an approved mechanic template rather than inventing an unrelated activity.
- Store the selected reward as structured state.
- Apply the reward to the next eligible pairing, seating, role, clue, or side-conversation event.
- Send the challenge separately to every participant while preserving identical operational facts.
- Use a visible timer only when timing is necessary.
- Support both self-reported completion and prototype host verification.
- Track cumulative structural advantages by participant.
- Support `completed`, `tie`, `cancelled`, and `fallback_used` outcomes.
- If no valid challenge or reward exists, skip the mechanic rather than creating a meaningless competition.

### First Impression Ranking

**ID:** `early_first_impression_ranking`

**Stage:** Early date

**Core purpose:**  
Creates immediate romantic tension by asking each participant to privately rank who they are most curious about before the group becomes too familiar.

**Best for:**  
Groups where all four participants are romantically compatible and the date has already included enough interaction for an initial read.

**Avoid when:**  
The group has only just arrived, romantic preference compatibility is incomplete, or a participant has opted out of ranking-based tasks.

**Mechanic:**  
Each participant privately ranks the other eligible participants based on first impression.

The ranking should focus on curiosity rather than attractiveness or long-term compatibility.

Suggested prompt:

> Based only on the date so far, rank the people you are most curious to know better.

Ditto records the rankings privately and reveals only selected outcomes.

Possible reveals:

- “Someone ranked you first.”
- “Two people chose the same person.”
- “Your first choice also placed you in their top two.”
- “There is one mutual first-place match.”
- “The group’s rankings are more divided than expected.”

Exact rankings should remain hidden unless the mechanic specifically calls for a later reveal.

**Task flow:**

1. Wait until the group has had meaningful initial interaction.
2. Send each participant a private ranking prompt.
3. Show only romantically eligible participants as options.
4. Require a complete ranking with no ties.
5. Store each response privately.
6. Calculate mutual interest, shared choices, and ranking patterns.
7. Release one controlled reveal to create tension.
8. Save the full ranking data for later pairing or reveal mechanics.

**Interaction rules:**

- Never show one participant’s full ranking to the group.
- Do not publicly announce who ranked last.
- Frame the task around curiosity, energy, or connection—not physical appearance.
- Participants should not be pressured to explain their choices.
- Do not repeat the ranking too frequently during the same date.
- Early rankings should not permanently determine the final outcome.
- Avoid making one participant feel visibly excluded.
- A reveal should create possibility without exposing rejection.

**Reveal logic:**

Prioritize reveals in this order:

1. Mutual first-place interest
2. Someone receiving a first-place vote
3. Two participants choosing the same person
4. A participant appearing in multiple top-two rankings
5. A vague group-level pattern if no positive individual reveal is available

Never reveal:

- Who received no first-place votes
- Who ranked someone last
- A one-sided rejection
- Exact ranking positions without consent

**Codex implementation notes:**

- Trigger only after the minimum early-interaction threshold is met.
- Filter ranking options using each participant’s dating preferences.
- Store rankings as private structured data.
- Calculate mutual first choices, top-two overlaps, and concentration of interest.
- Select the safest positive reveal using the reveal priority rules.
- Do not generate a negative reveal when no positive pattern exists.
- Make ranking data available to later mechanics such as pair selection, side conversations, anonymous interest reveals, and the final choice.
- Prevent participants from viewing one another’s submissions.

### Secret Question Drop

**ID:** `early_secret_question_drop`

**Stage:** Early date

**Core purpose:**  
Lets participants ask something they are genuinely curious about without having to publicly claim the question, creating intrigue while keeping the conversation natural.

**Best for:**  
Groups that have completed introductions and need a stronger conversation prompt without forcing an overly serious or romantic moment.

**Avoid when:**  
The group is still visibly uncomfortable, participants have not interacted enough to form genuine curiosity, or the available questions would expose sensitive application information.

**Mechanic:**  
Each participant privately submits one question they want someone in the group to answer.

The question can be directed at:

- One specific eligible participant
- Anyone in the group
- The person they are currently paired with

Ditto removes the sender’s identity, screens the question, and delivers approved questions one at a time.

Suggested prompt:

> Drop one question you actually want answered.  
> They’ll see the question, but not who sent it.

The receiver answers aloud to the group.

Ditto may later reveal limited information about the sender, but should not immediately expose their identity.

**Question examples:**

- What was your honest first impression of this group?
- What kind of person usually catches your attention?
- What is something people misunderstand about you at first?
- When do you know you are actually interested in someone?
- What is the most spontaneous thing you have done recently?
- What would make tonight feel like a successful date?
- What is one personality trait you cannot fake liking?

**Task flow:**

1. Send each participant a private question-submission prompt.
2. Allow them to select a recipient or choose “anyone.”
3. Screen every question before delivery.
4. Reject or rewrite questions that are unsafe, humiliating, invasive, or too explicit.
5. Select the strongest approved questions.
6. Deliver one question at a time without naming the sender.
7. Ask the recipient to answer aloud.
8. Optionally save the sender identity for a later reveal.

**Interaction rules:**

- Questions should create curiosity, not interrogation.
- Do not allow questions about trauma, income, body count, medical history, family conflict, immigration status, or other sensitive personal information.
- Do not deliver questions designed to embarrass, expose, or test someone.
- Avoid generic prompts that could have been asked before the date.
- Do not send multiple questions to the same participant while another participant receives none.
- The sender must remain anonymous during the initial answer.
- Participants may skip a question without penalty.
- Do not automatically reveal the sender after the answer.

The group has not interacted enough, public selection could clearly isolate someone, or participants opted into private choices only.

**Mechanic:**  
Each participant privately submits who they would most like to be paired with for the final activity.

Ditto collects all selections before revealing any result.

The system then forms the strongest valid pair configuration using:

- Mutual selections
- Romantic compatibility
- Pairing history
- One-on-one time balance
- Participant boundaries
- Previous declines
- Remaining valid pairings

The final pairings are announced together.

Suggested private prompt:

> Final pairing.
>
> Who do you want beside you for the next round?
>
> Your choice stays private unless it creates a match.

Suggested group reveal:

> final pairs just dropped:
>
> Jacob + Olivia  
> Maya + Ethan
>
> one last task.

Ditto does not reveal who selected whom unless a later mechanic explicitly allows it.

**Task flow:**

1. Wait until every participant has experienced multiple group or paired interactions.
2. Send the recoupling choice privately to each participant.
3. Show only romantically eligible participants.
4. Allow one selection or “Let Ditto choose.”
5. Store all choices privately.
6. Identify mutual selections.
7. Generate all valid two-pair configurations.
8. Score each configuration using mutuality, preference strength, balance, and pairing history.
9. Select the strongest safe configuration.
10. Announce both final pairs at the same time.
11. Assign each pair the same final activity or equivalent roles.
12. Store the final pairing and selection data for the closing mechanic.

**Interaction rules:**

- Choices must be submitted privately.
- Do not make participants choose aloud.
- Do not announce who was selected first, last, or not at all.
- Do not leave one participant publicly standing alone.
- Do not reveal one-sided selections.
- Do not reveal how many people chose each participant.
- Do not allow participants to select themselves.
- Only show romantically compatible options.
- Participants may choose “Let Ditto choose” without penalty.
- Do not frame the result as an official couple.
- Recoupling affects only the final activity.
- It does not automatically determine the Final Signal or second-date result.

**Mutual selection logic:**

Prioritize a mutual selection when:

- Both participants selected each other
- Both remain romantically compatible
- Neither has declined or opted out of the pairing
- The pair has not already dominated the date
- The remaining two participants form a valid pairing
- The final task supports both resulting pairs

A mutual selection should receive strong weighting but should not automatically override safety, balance, or compatibility rules.

**One-sided selection handling:**

When a selection is not mutual:

- Keep it completely private.
- Do not notify the selected participant.
- Do not describe the result as rejection.
- Use the selection as a moderate preference signal.
- Pair the participants only when the overall configuration remains appropriate.
- Do not force the selected person into the pairing solely because someone chose them.

**Configuration logic:**

For a four-person group, Ditto should evaluate all valid two-pair configurations.

Score each configuration using:

1. Number of mutual selections
2. Total preference alignment
3. Romantic compatibility
4. Underexplored connection potential
5. Previous pairing repetition
6. Total one-on-one time balance
7. Decline and opt-out history
8. Final-task suitability

Apply penalties when:

- One pair has already spent disproportionate time together
- The configuration recreates the immediately previous pairings
- A participant has previously declined that pairing
- One participant repeatedly receives no meaningful choice
- The remaining pair appears to be formed only by default
- The configuration violates romantic compatibility

**No mutual selection logic:**

If there are no mutual selections:

- Choose the configuration with the strongest combined preference alignment.
- Prefer pairs that have plausible compatibility and limited previous interaction.
- Avoid presenting the result as mutual.
- Do not reveal that no mutual selections occurred.
- Continue with a neutral pairing announcement.

**Conflicting selection logic:**

If several participants select the same person:

- Do not reveal the conflict.
- Do not show selection counts.
- Prioritize mutuality first.
- Then consider preference alignment, pairing balance, and previous interaction time.
- Ensure the final configuration remains fair to the full group.
- Do not repeatedly reward the most selected participant with complete control.

**Let Ditto choose logic:**

If a participant selects “Let Ditto choose”:

- Treat it as openness to any eligible pairing.
- Do not interpret it as equal romantic interest in everyone.
- Use compatibility, pairing history, and balance data.
- Do not reveal who selected this option.

**Final activity logic:**

The recoupled pairs should complete one shared final activity.

Approved examples:

- Prepare or share the final course
- Take a final pair photo
- Complete a short collaborative challenge
- Choose one item for the other pair
- Create a short memory from the date
- Complete a paired conversation walk
- Build or finish one part of a shared object
- Select a song that represents the night

Avoid:

- Forced physical affection
- Public attraction rankings
- Confessions
- Kissing instructions
- Comparing which pair looks more legitimate
- Tasks that make the unchosen result obvious
- Questions requiring participants to reveal their private selection

**Codex implementation notes:**

- Deliver the choice through each participant’s private Ditto DM.
- Filter options using romantic compatibility, opt-outs, and active-session status.
- Support `participant_id` and `ditto_choose` response types.
- Store every submission as hidden structured data.
- Generate every valid two-pair configuration.
- Score configurations using mutuality, preference alignment, compatibility, repetition, balance, and decline history.
- Announce both final pairs simultaneously.
- Never expose individual choices, selection counts, or one-sided interest.
- Store the selected configuration as `recoupling`.
- Preserve all private choice data for Final Signal analysis without allowing it to determine the final result automatically.
- Support a `no_valid_configuration` fallback using a four-person group activity.

### Someone Chose You Reveal

**ID:** `late_someone_chose_you_reveal`

**Stage:** Late date

**Core purpose:**  
Creates romantic tension by confirming that a participant received genuine interest, while protecting the chooser’s identity and avoiding public rejection.

**Best for:**  
Dates where private rankings, recoupling choices, side-conversation requests, or other clear interest signals have already been collected.

**Avoid when:**  
There is no genuine supporting signal, the reveal would make the sender obvious, or one participant has already received significantly more validation than everyone else.

**Mechanic:**  
Ditto privately tells a participant that someone selected them during an earlier private mechanic.

Possible supporting signals include:

- They were ranked first
- Someone requested a side conversation with them
- Someone selected them during Recoupling
- Someone chose them in a final curiosity prompt
- Someone repeatedly selected them across multiple mechanics

Example private message:

The sender’s identity remains hidden.

The participant may receive one optional response:

- I think I know who
- I have someone in mind too
- Give me a vague hint
- Keep it anonymous

The response stays private.

**Task flow:**

1. Review stored private interest signals.
2. Identify a participant who received a valid selection.
3. Confirm that the reveal is positive, balanced, and sufficiently anonymous.
4. Send the reveal privately.
5. Offer an optional private response.
6. Store the participant’s response.
7. Use the response only as supporting data for later closing mechanics.
8. Keep the chooser’s identity hidden unless a later mutual reveal explicitly permits disclosure.

**Interaction rules:**

- Never invent a selection.
- Do not reveal the chooser’s identity.
- Do not reveal the exact mechanic where the selection occurred when that would expose the chooser.
- Do not reveal how many people selected the participant.
- Do not tell anyone that they received no selections.
- Do not frame the reveal as proof of a match or guaranteed second date.
- Do not use stronger language than the stored signal supports.
- Do not say someone is “in love,” “obsessed,” or definitely romantically interested.
- Do not pressure the recipient to identify the chooser.
- Do not send the recipient’s reaction to the chooser.
- Keep the message private.
- Do not send several validation reveals to one participant while others receive none.

**Signal priority logic:**

Prioritize signals in this order:

1. Mutual first-place selection
2. Mutual Recoupling selection
3. Direct side-conversation request
4. First-place ranking
5. Repeated interest across multiple mechanics
6. Single late-date choice

The reveal should only trigger when the signal meets a minimum confidence threshold.

Do not trigger when:

- The signal was withdrawn or superseded
- The selection was made only because no other valid option existed
- The sender later opted out of the pairing
- The reveal would expose a one-sided rejection elsewhere
- The recipient has opted out of romantic reveals
- The sender would be immediately identifiable
- The signal is based only on appearance

**Anonymity logic:**

Before sending, Ditto should check whether the recipient could identify the chooser with near certainty.

Factors that may make the sender too obvious include:

- Only one romantically eligible participant exists
- The recipient had only one previous pairing
- The selection followed a visible participant-controlled action
- The group already knows who requested the interaction
- A hint would leave only one possible sender

When anonymity cannot be protected:

- Skip the reveal
- Delay it until the closing result
- Use a broader group-level message instead

Approved group-level fallback:

> At least one choice tonight was mutual.

Do not name the participants.

**Optional hint logic:**

If the participant selects “Give me a vague hint,” Ditto may send one non-identifying clue.

Approved hints:

- You spoke with them one-on-one tonight.
- They noticed you earlier than you realized.
- Their choice changed during the date.
- You were not their most obvious first impression.
- You completed a paired task together.
- They chose you more than once.

Do not reveal:

- Gender
- Clothing
- Seat position
- Initials
- Exact ranking
- Exact submission wording
- The name of the mechanic
- Any clue that identifies one person with certainty

**Balance logic:**

- Limit each participant to one reveal of this type per date.
- Prioritize mutual or meaningful signals.
- Avoid revealing only to the most selected participant.
- Do not manufacture reveals to make distribution equal.
- Skip the mechanic when a safe, genuine reveal is unavailable.
- Consider whether another participant has received little positive reinforcement before selecting the recipient.
- Do not reveal selection counts or compare popularity.

**Relationship to other mechanics:**

This reveal may use data from:

- First Impression Ranking
- Private Side Conversation Request
- Curiosity Ping
- Recoupling
- Anonymous Compliment Drop
- Final preference selection

It must not expose the private contents of those mechanics.

This reveal does not automatically:

- Create a side conversation
- Change the final pairing
- Reveal mutuality
- Guarantee a second date
- Replace the Final Signal

**Codex implementation notes:**

- Read from ranking, recoupling, side-conversation request, curiosity, and final-choice data.
- Assign each candidate reveal a confidence score.
- Apply authenticity, balance, anonymity, opt-out, and repetition checks.
- Deliver the reveal only through the recipient’s private Ditto DM.
- Support `think_i_know`, `someone_in_mind`, `request_hint`, and `keep_anonymous` responses.
- Store responses as hidden structured data.
- Never forward the recipient’s response to the chooser.
- Select hints only from approved templates.
- Run an anonymity check before sending any hint.
- Support `skip_reveal` and `group_level_fallback` outcomes.
- Never expose selection counts, sender identity, or the original private response.

### Final Signal

**ID:** `closing_final_signal`

**Active prototype constraint:** The beach-cookout prototype accepts exactly one group-member name or “no one” through plain text and permanently locks the first valid submission. Generalized variants elsewhere in this library that mention secondary choices, “not sure,” edits, deadline replacement, multiple mutual matches, or pre-reveal withdrawal do not apply to this prototype.

**Stage:** Closing

**Core purpose:**  
Captures each participant’s final romantic interest privately after the date and reveals only mutual choices at 12:00 AM following the date.

**Best for:**  
Dates where participants have completed the main group experience and had enough interaction to decide whether they want to see someone again.

**Avoid when:**  
The date ends unexpectedly early, a participant leaves before meaningful interaction occurs, or the session is cancelled.

**Mechanic:**  
At the end of the date, Ditto privately asks each participant whether they want to see anyone from the group again.

Suggested prompt:

> final signal.
>
> who would you actually want to see again?

Each participant sends one group member’s name or “no one” as plain text. Matching is trimmed and case-insensitive. Self-selection and invalid names receive a brief conversational validation response.

All choices remain private.

No result is revealed immediately after submission.

At 12:00 AM following the date, Ditto compares all submitted choices and reveals only mutual selections.

One-sided selections are never exposed.

**Timing logic:**

- Send the Final Signal prompt near the end of the date.
- Lock each participant’s response permanently when it is submitted.
- Do not reveal results immediately.
- Reveal eligible mutual results at 12:00 AM following the date.
- Use the date session’s local timezone.
- Treat 12:00 AM as the start of the calendar day immediately after the date began.
- If the date continues past 12:00 AM, delay the reveal until the date session is marked complete.
- If the date ends after 12:00 AM, reveal results shortly after session completion rather than during the date.
- Do not provide edit, undo, reopen, secondary-choice, or save-for-later states.

Example:

- Date begins Saturday at 2:00 PM
- Date ends Saturday at 5:00 PM
- Final Signal is submitted before the deadline
- Result is revealed Sunday at 12:00 AM

**Task flow:**

1. Wait until the main date experience is nearly complete.
2. Send the Final Signal prompt privately to every active participant.
3. Accept one group-member name or “no one” through plain text input.
4. Validate the response and reject self-selection.
5. Store the response privately and lock it immediately.
6. Wait until the scheduled 12:00 AM reveal time.
7. Compare all locked choices.
8. Identify valid mutual selections.
9. Run safety, withdrawal, and eligibility checks.
10. Deliver the appropriate result privately to each participant.
11. Do not reveal one-sided interest.
12. Mark the Final Signal result as delivered.

**Interaction rules:**

- Every choice must remain private.
- Do not ask participants to submit their choice aloud.
- Do not reveal results before the scheduled reveal time.
- Do not reveal who selected whom unless the selection is mutual.
- Do not reveal how many people selected a participant.
- Do not announce who received no selections.
- Do not frame “No one” as failure.
- Do not pressure participants to choose someone.
- Do not require participants to explain their choice.
- Do not allow participants to select themselves.
- Validate mutual romantic eligibility when resolving a mutual result.
- Earlier rankings or pairings must not automatically become the final choice.
- Results must be delivered privately rather than through a shared group message.

**Selection format:**

The default format is one plain text input accepting:

- One group member’s name
- No one

Suggested prompt:

> final signal.
>
> who would you want to see again after tonight?
>
> send me one name—or send “no one.”

**Submission lock logic:**

- Lock the first valid submission immediately and permanently.
- Preserve that locked submission across POV switching and simulation advancement.
- Do not expose an edit, undo, reset, or replacement action.
- Do not show whether another participant has already submitted.
- A missing submission should not be interpreted as “No one.”
- Mark missing submissions as `no_response`.

**Mutual match logic:**

A mutual result occurs when:

- Participant A selects Participant B
- Participant B selects Participant A
- Both selections were submitted before the deadline
- Both choices remain valid
- Neither participant withdrew
- Both participants are romantically compatible
- Both completed the date session
- No safety or moderation restriction blocks the connection

A mutual result requires both participants’ single locked choices to point to one another.

**Mutual result delivery:**

When two participants select each other, notify both privately at the scheduled reveal time.

Example:

> your final signal came back mutual.
>
> you and Olivia both chose each other.
>
> want Ditto to help with date two?

Approved actions:

- Help plan date two
- Exchange contact details
- Keep communication in Ditto
- Not right now

Do not automatically share contact information without consent from both participants.

**One-sided result handling:**

When a participant selects someone who does not select them:

- Do not reveal that the selection was one-sided.
- Do not identify the other participant’s choice.
- Do not describe the result as rejection.
- Do not say that the other participant did not choose them.
- Do not reveal whether the other participant selected someone else.
- Deliver a neutral result at the scheduled reveal time.

Example:

> the final signals are in.
>
> no mutual reveal this time.

Avoid language that suggests blame, popularity, failure, or inadequacy.

**No selection handling:**

If a participant selects “No one”:

- Store the response privately.
- Do not reveal it to the group.
- Do not notify anyone who selected them.
- Do not lower their future matching score.
- Do not pressure them to reconsider.
- Deliver a neutral confirmation after submission.

Example:

> locked. no explanation needed.

At the scheduled result time, send a simple session-closing message.

**Not sure handling:**

If a participant selects “Not sure yet”:

- Store the response as unresolved.
- Do not count it as a mutual selection.
- Do not reveal that another participant selected them.
- Do not pressure them to decide immediately.
- Treat the response as final once the deadline passes unless the product supports a separate extension window.

Example:

> totally fair.
>
> your signal is staying unresolved.

Any extension window must have a clear expiration time and apply consistently.

**No response handling:**

If a participant does not submit before the deadline:

- Mark the response as `no_response`.
- Do not interpret it as rejection.
- Do not create a mutual result involving that participant.
- Do not reveal that they failed to submit.
- Send a neutral closing message if appropriate.

**Multiple mutual selections:**

If one participant has mutual selections with more than one person:

- Do not publicly compare the matches.
- Notify the participant privately.
- Do not reveal one mutual connection to the other.
- Allow the participant to remain open to both unless the product requires one connection.
- Do not force an immediate ranking unless required for logistics.
- Require separate consent before sharing contact details with each person.

Example:

> more than one final signal came back mutual.
>
> you can decide what you want to do next with each connection privately.

**Result priority logic:**

Prioritize outcomes in this order:

1. Mutual primary-primary selection
2. Mutual primary-secondary selection
3. Mutual secondary-secondary selection
4. Multiple mutual selections
5. No mutual result
6. Unresolved result
7. No response
8. Invalidated result

Do not reveal these internal categories to participants.

**Relationship to earlier mechanics:**

Final Signal may consider earlier data for safety and context, but the result must come from the participant’s submitted closing choice.

Earlier data may include:

- First Impression Ranking
- Private Side Conversation Request
- Curiosity Ping
- Ten-Minute Side Conversation
- Recoupling
- Someone Chose You Reveal

Earlier data must not:

- Automatically submit a Final Signal
- Override the participant’s final choice
- Create a false mutual result
- Be revealed during the final outcome
- Be used to pressure someone into choosing a previous pairing

**Contact-sharing logic:**

Contact information may be shared only when:

- A valid mutual selection exists
- Both participants explicitly consent
- Neither participant has withdrawn
- No moderation or safety restriction applies

Approved options:

- Share Instagram handles
- Share phone numbers
- Keep communication inside Ditto
- Ask Ditto to arrange another date

Do not expose private contact information before both participants approve the same method.

**Second-date logic:**

When both participants want help planning another date, Ditto may:

1. Ask each participant privately for availability.
2. Ask for activity preferences.
3. Identify overlapping times.
4. Suggest one date plan.
5. Confirm the plan with both participants.
6. Share details only after both approve.

A mutual Final Signal does not automatically create a calendar event, reservation, or contact exchange.

**Safety and withdrawal logic:**

Before delivering a mutual result, verify:

- Both participants are still active
- Neither participant has blocked or reported the other
- Neither participant has withdrawn their selection
- No safety flag requires review
- Contact sharing remains permitted

Participants may withdraw their selection before the reveal time.

If a result becomes invalid:

- Do not disclose the original mutual selection.
- Send a neutral closing message.
- Do not explain another participant’s private safety action.
- Store the invalidated result securely.

- Rename the mechanic from `Final Signal` to `Final Signal`.
- Use the ID `closing_final_signal`.
- Deliver the prompt through each participant’s private Ditto DM.

- Do not say that they received no interest.
- Send a neutral private closing message at the scheduled reveal time.

Example:

> the final signals are in.
>
> no mutual reveal this time.

Or:

> your date is officially closed.
>
> thanks for showing up honestly.

Avoid overly cheerful copy that may feel dismissive.

**Withdrawal logic:**

Participants may withdraw their Final Signal before the reveal is delivered.

If either participant withdraws:

- Invalidate the mutual result.
- Do not reveal that a mutual selection previously existed.
- Do not notify the other participant that someone withdrew.
- Deliver a neutral closing message instead.
- Store the invalidation privately.

After a mutual reveal is delivered, participants may still decline any next step.

Declining the next step does not erase the fact that the original Final Signal was mutual.

**Safety logic:**

Before delivering the reveal, check:

- Block status
- Report status
- Safety flags
- Moderation restrictions
- Session completion
- Participant eligibility
- Withdrawal status
- Contact-sharing restrictions

If a safety issue invalidates the reveal:

- Do not disclose the mutual result.
- Do not explain the specific safety action.
- Send a neutral closing message.
- Prevent contact sharing and date planning.
- Store the result as `invalidated`.

**Relationship to Final Signal:**

Mutual Match Reveal depends entirely on Final Signal.

Final Signal:

- Collects participant choices
- Allows edits before the deadline
- Locks responses
- Schedules result processing

Mutual Match Reveal:

- Processes the locked responses
- Confirms valid mutuality
- Delivers results at the scheduled time
- Handles next-step consent
- Protects all one-sided choices

Do not combine these mechanics into one immediate interaction.
